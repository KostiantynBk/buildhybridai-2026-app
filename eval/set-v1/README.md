# Synthetic set v1

These are invented test cases, not real people, client records or real projects. Any similarity is coincidental. Public product names in the copied practice/profile data are ordinary references, not endorsements.

`profiles.json`, `dev-examples.json`, and the five versioned prompt texts were written on 9–11 November 2026 before the submission period and copied from the planning materials. The 20 scored notes, their labels and the six change pairs were newly written during the submission period. Practice notes and their labels are not scored. Simulated-session inputs are separate and were not copied into this set.

Each scored note is one invented paragraph of 80–250 words. At least six labeled notes and one of the three unlabeled brainstorms have 150–250 words. Label a statement only if the plan relies on it and being wrong would cost time or money: an **assumption** is a belief about a price, limit or service/user behavior; a **decision** is a choice already made; a **constraint** is a fixed limit or externally imposed input or acceptance requirement; an **open-question** is an acknowledged unknown whose answer changes the build or spending. Do not label wishes, feature ideas, visual or wording preferences, proposed future tests, or current state without a commitment. Each span is verbatim and nonoverlapping, with fixed topics and at most four labels per note. Three pure brainstorms deliberately have no labels.

For the two earlier borderline cases, n03 now explicitly treats international card acceptance as a customer requirement and the one-price/conversion plan as a settled decision. In n06, the monthly offer is a settled decision and whether pay-per-use would be better remains an open question. In n15, the receiving tool's date format is an external input constraint. The labelled blind spots and private-token strings are for later tests, not Run 1 model inputs. Private tokens cover invented people's names, client organizations and explicit money amounts; n17's exact client date and weekly capacity are not private under this fixed rule. Exact matching does not guarantee detection of paraphrases or other identifiers.

Each change pair identifies the old label with `targetSpan` and states its purpose in `changeDescription`. Three pairs change the target's meaning without changing other labeled items; three do not change any labeled item's meaning. Of the latter, n11 rewords the target without changing its meaning.

Known weaknesses: a small hand-written set, imperfect or incomplete labels, overlapping topics, repeated narrative structure, and potentially coincidental similarities to real names. Precision counts only labelled items and may penalize a valid but unlabelled extraction. Human review of all labels and the specified sample of unmatched items is necessary. The set is not evidence of real user demand or model reliability beyond these cases.
